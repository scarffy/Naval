# Assassin's Creed trading minigames: research notes

Why this exists: Naval started life inspired by the convoy minigame in Assassin's Creed III (2012). This note re-learns how that minigame worked, then looks at its two descendants, Black Flag's Kenway's Fleet (2013) and Rogue's fleet (2014). It ends with a comparison against Naval's current design. Naval is an original game, so the purpose is to understand the source and take ideas, not to copy it.

How reliable this is: web pages could not be fetched, so everything here comes from search-result summaries of fan wikis, forums and guides. No official Ubisoft documentation turned up. Figures are community-reported. Anything I could not confirm is marked "(unverified)". Where sources disagree, the disagreement is stated. The 2026 remake "Black Flag Resynced" shows up in many results. It rebuilt the fleet system, so it is not the source here and is mentioned only to avoid confusion.

## 1. Assassin's Creed III: Homestead and convoys

### 1.1 The loop in one paragraph

Connor's Homestead is a settlement that he fills with residents (artisans and helpers). Raw materials (pelts, ore, lumber, food) are bought through an "Accounting Book" interface and turned into finished goods by those artisans. The finished goods are loaded into convoys, either wagons (land) or ships (naval), and sent to merchants in other places. The convoy returns after a delay with money. Risk of loss, travel time, destination prices and convoy capacity all feed into how much you make. Prima's guide describes the Homestead as the main money engine of the game, because story missions and most side missions pay no cash. Its expensive upgrades are described as effectively unaffordable unless the player trades through convoys.

### 1.2 Step by step, as a player sees it

1. **Unlock the Homestead.** It opens late in Sequence 5. Residents then arrive gradually through side missions tied to white Homestead icons. One achievement guide counts about 10 extra characters or couples who can be persuaded to settle. A GamesRadar guide counts 37 Homestead missions in total.
2. **Recruit artisans.** The trades named in guides are blacksmith, tailor, woodworker, huntress (also called hunter), lumberer, miner, plus a doctor and a priest who do other jobs. Rough timing reported: blacksmith around Sequence 8 (found in the Frontier in summer), tailor, woodworker and huntress around Sequence 9. Sources differ on exact sequences, so treat these as approximate.
3. **Level the artisans.** Each artisan has levels, raised by doing their side missions. Higher levels unlock more recipes. One breakdown lists recipe counts per level (blacksmith about 11, 12, 15, 12 across levels 1 to 4; tailor 15, 15, 8 across levels 1 to 3; woodworker 12, 11, 6 across levels 1 to 3). SegmentNext says the full recipe book has 131 recipes in eleven categories. Some recipes are found in the world (chests, loose pickups) or discovered by experimenting.
4. **Open the Accounting Book.** It is available at the manor study, and also at static general stores in Boston, New York and the Frontier. It does not work at moving peddlers. Everything economic happens through this one book: buying materials, crafting, and dispatching convoys.
5. **Buy raw materials from the stockpile tab.** The Homestead's residents supply raw goods. One guide says the stock is capped at 10 per item, and refills slowly, about one unit every two minutes (single-source claim, unverified). Prices are fixed per item. An example from the fandom wiki's price table: apples cost 6 to buy and have separate sale values by land and by sea (21 and 37 in that example, unverified beyond one snippet).
6. **Craft.** Choose an artisan, then a recipe. Crafting is instant from the player's view. A Hidden Blade guide reports that the artisan's pay per craft rises with how many crafts that artisan has done recently, starting at 25 pounds and rising by 25 each time (single source, unverified), and that you should craft the most complex items first. This works as a mild brake on mass-producing cheap goods.
7. **Fill a convoy.** A convoy has a number of slots. You assign crafted or hunted goods to them.
8. **Pick a destination and send it.** The destination list shows a merchant, a travel time and a risk figure, and the expected payout. A shop only appears as a destination after the player has visited it once.
9. **Wait.** The convoy is away for a while and then the money arrives. If it was attacked, it is lost and you are told it was lost.
10. **Replace or grow.** You buy more convoys and capacity upgrades through the crafting tab ("Special Items"), and spend the profits on Homestead upgrades and gear.

### 1.3 Land convoys (wagons)

- You craft a land convoy from the ledger's Special Items. One Steam guide says up to 3 can be owned; another source repeats "3 max". Whether that cap covers land only or all convoys is unclear.
- Capacity: a guide recalls 3 or 4 slots at the start, rising to 8 with a capacity upgrade. A forum thread asked whether the upgrade does anything, which suggests the upgrade was not obvious in the UI. New capacity upgrades apply to convoys you already own.
- Destinations: the shops in Boston, New York and the Frontier. A trophy ("Monopoly Man") requires sending a convoy to each of Boston, New York and the Frontier. Each location has three general stores. Examples with travel times from a Hidden Blade guide: Hancock's Store No. 5 in Boston at 12 minutes, and in New York Rhinelander's Sugars, De Lancey's Imports and Van Burgh's Spices at 22 minutes each (one source, unverified).
- Merchants pay different multiples of the base price. A Neoseeker poster reported Hancock's Store No. 5 paying about 3.5 times the shop price for Fine Clothes (base 305). A full 8-slot wagon of them was said to net about 8,540 pounds (base value 2,440, then multiplied). Another example pairs a New York wagon worth about 3,000 pounds with a Boston one worth about 2,100 per trip. All of these are forum figures.
- Taxes: Prima's list of fort rewards says liberating Fort St-Mathieu lowers taxes on land convoys. That confirms a land-convoy tax exists, but no tax rate was found.
- Risk on land: sources are thin. Liberating the Boston district North Boston is reported to lower the convoy risk factor (Prima's list). The 25% risk drop seen on naval routes is not reported for land.

### 1.4 Naval convoys (ships)

- Unlock: you craft the convoy ship from the ledger. GameFAQs says this needs the woodworker Lance at level 2, and the recipe uses Spruce Lumber, Oak Bark and Bear Grease. Capacity upgrade comes from Lance at level 3 (Maple Lumber and Kindling, per the fandom wiki). The naval convoy appears after Sequence 6 or so.
- Capacity: one Steam guide says a maximum of 15 slots after upgrades. A forum thread says "12 or 15". Ubisoft support is quoted (about the Remastered version) as saying the upgrade adds 1 slot. Treat 15 as the likely ceiling and 12 as a possible alternative.
- Count: one guide says up to 3 naval convoys, and its own author could not confirm that. Others say 2. Unresolved.
- Destinations: the naval routes are overseas colonial and Caribbean markets, not the three land cities. The routes named in community posts are the Bahamas, the Virgin Islands and Saint Augustine (Florida). The Virgin Islands route is described as the most valuable. I could not find a full list or per-route travel times (Havana, Nassau and Charleston did not appear as AC3 destinations in any result, so do not assume them).
- Example earnings: a commonly cited trick is ten perfect bear pelts sent to Saint Augustine, estimated at about 15,000 pounds per trip, after clearing the three naval missions on that route (a guide blog, unverified).

### 1.5 Route risk

This is the best-documented part.

- **What it is:** each naval route has a risk rate that decides how likely a convoy is attacked and lost. It is shown as a percentage in the Accounting Book. Longer routes pay more but take longer and are riskier, so the player trades travel time, risk and payout against each other.
- **Starting value:** routes start high. One wiki reports 80%. 
- **What lowers it:** Privateer missions (naval side missions tied to each route). There are three per route, and each completed mission cuts the route's risk by 25 percentage points. Three missions take an 80% route down to 5%. One commenter states 5% is the floor (so it never reaches zero).
- **Known bug:** several players reported the Bahamas and Virgin Islands routes staying at 80% after all missions were done. Some said it never recurred on another playthrough, others said it was in the original and never fixed in the Remaster.
- **Other lowering factors:** liberating forts and Boston districts is reported to reduce convoy risk or taxes. One forum post hedges on whether forts affect risk, so treat it as likely but unconfirmed.
- **When a convoy is lost:** the game just reports that it was lost at sea. Players found no way to defend a naval convoy once attacked. You lose the goods and the convoy, and can craft a replacement. Land convoys are different: they are physically attacked in the Frontier by Redcoats, and the player can run there and defend them (looking for a shield icon), though whether that is required for a land convoy to survive is not clear from the results.
- **Takeaway:** the risk number is readable, and the player has a specific action, with a bounded set of tasks, that reduces it. That is a strong design pattern.

### 1.6 Prices and travel time

- **Prices:** buying prices at the Homestead are fixed. Selling prices are fixed per merchant and per good. The Hidden Blade "Economic Encyclopedia" describes each merchant as applying a different multiplier to a good's base value, and says merchants change what they will pay (so prices may rotate or vary, wording unclear). I found no evidence of a supply and demand market that moves when you sell. Selling in person at a general store pays base price, while convoys pay a multiple of it. That multiple is the whole point of convoys.
- **Travel time:** real time, not in-game time. Reported land trips are around 12 to 22 minutes, in the Hidden Blade figures above. Naval trips were not found. A guide blog suggests running the convoy every 12 minutes for a full land convoy upgrade. This implies convoys run while the player does other things.

### 1.7 Money, scale and progression

- Early-game profit example from a fan blog: beaver pelts about 1,600 pounds per buy-and-sell loop early, up to about 4,100 with the full land convoy upgrade every 12 minutes (unverified).
- Late-game examples reach several thousand per land wagon and about 15,000 per naval trip with top goods.
- Prima says most story missions and side quests pay no money, so the economy is the main income, and the big Homestead upgrades are priced assuming you use it.
- Progression: more residents lead to more recipes, which leads to higher-value goods. More convoys and larger capacity lead to more goods per cycle. Clearing naval routes leads to lower risk and more profitable routes. Fort liberation leads to lower taxes. The player scales both the value per slot and the number of slots.

### 1.8 What the system lacks

- No supply and demand movement found, so no price pressure from big shipments.
- No ship stats on naval convoys beyond capacity.
- No choice of crew, captain or ship type.
- No visible market news.
- A single unlock chain of items rather than open exploration of trade networks.

## 2. Black Flag: Kenway's Fleet

### 2.1 Overview

Kenway's Fleet is a layer of management above the sailing game. You capture enemy ships by boarding them in naval combat, choose "send to Kenway's Fleet", and then manage them from the map table in the Jackdaw's captain's cabin. Ships go off on trade and combat missions in the background and return with money. In the 2013 original, the fleet was tied to online features and a companion app (sources vary on whether it was strictly required, with a Game Informer item about an online pass for some single-player content). It unlocks early in Sequence 4.

### 2.2 Ships and capacity

- You start with a few ships, up to 15 in the original after unlocking docks (one source). Dock slots need to be unlocked, and a captured ship with no free dock can be dismissed or replaced.
- Ship classes and cargo, as reported by wiki and forum posts: Schooner about 15 to 20 cargo, fast, weak guns; Brig about 25 to 35; Frigate about 40 to 50 (one claim of 60 disputed); Man-o'-War about 70 to 90, with HP around 135 to 150. Each class comes in levels, and higher levels carry more.
- Mission needs a minimum cargo, so some missions can only be done by a big ship. One post mentions a mission listed as needing 30 but actually needing 60, so the UI may have been unreliable (anecdotal).
- Ship stats are partly random from what you capture, so the fleet is built by luck and effort rather than by shopping.

### 2.3 The map, regions and routes

- The fleet map is split into regions and trade routes. Missions are listed as routes between points, each showing a base real-time duration, a reward and a danger colour.
- Danger levels (original): green safe, yellow hazardous, orange dangerous, red treacherous. They rise by one level every few hours of real time if left alone.
- Lowering danger: fight a ship battle on the route from the route menu. Winning lowers the danger by one level. The route's danger therefore needs ongoing maintenance.
- Specific region names were not confirmed in the results. (A Steam guide gives route timings for North Africa, Cape Verde and the South Atlantic, see below.)

### 2.4 The battle minigame

- It is a simple turn-based game where your fleet's ships take part and each ship gives bonuses. Guides say it is easy to learn, and that using heavy ships ("liners") makes losing almost impossible. After each battle the ships need repairs, which cost resources (one guide says gems, unverified). The exact turn rules (a card or board game) were not found.
- Players often called it simple and without interactive battles.

### 2.5 Durations and rewards

- Real-time. A Steam guide lists: North Africa 6 to 10 hours, paying 1,000 to 2,000 reales; Cape Verde 10 to 24 hours for 1,600 to 3,400; South Atlantic 5 to 10 hours for 800 to 1,400. One forum reply confirms the missions are real-time.
- Many missions give a fixed amount of reales, and some give a one-time special reward such as a treasure map or cosmetic Jackdaw upgrade.
- The money feeds Jackdaw upgrades, which let the player take on higher-rank ships.
- In the remake, missions run 30 to 60 minutes and pay roughly 1,250 to 4,600 reales (a different system, not the source).

### 2.6 Losses, upgrades, repair

- Ships can be lost in failed missions. Results did not give clear rules for the original, so treat loss rules as unverified.
- Repairs are required after battles. Upgrades to the Jackdaw come from the money, not from fleet ship upgrades.

### 2.7 Reception in brief

Mixed. Detail in section 4.

## 3. Rogue's fleet (short)

Rogue's fleet system is a close cousin of Black Flag's, from the same team. Differences found:

- Unlocks late in Sequence 3, from the captain's cabin on the Morrigan, and also from captured forts, headquarters and the New York home.
- Each ship sent runs a timer, and results depend on its stats. Smaller, faster ships finish sooner. Larger ships suit high-risk missions but are slower. Men-o'-war raise the chance of success a lot. Gunboats are recommended as useful additions.
- The mission list re-randomises every time the screen is opened, so the player can reroll to find an easier mission.
- Rewards are money (for example Louisiana is 720 pounds in one list) and some cosmetic items. Missions can be repeated for money but give no achievements.
- Rogue is purely about money. No separate battle minigame was mentioned in the results (unverified).
- The sailing is the same as Black Flag. The setting is North Atlantic, rivers and New York. A forum post claims Rogue's fleet has no ship blueprint element (unverified).
- Not found: mission timers in hours, loss rules, fleet size.

## 4. What players thought

AC3 Homestead and convoys:
- Guides call it the main source of money and one of the most consistent. SegmentNext says convoys can make a lot of money if you craft high-quality goods and sell them far away.
- Some reviewers listed "economy feels a bit pointless" and called the Homestead a good distraction. One user said only the Homestead naval and Benedict Arnold quests were worth doing.
- Softpedia argued Ubisoft made economy and combat too complex. An alleged developer comment says the Homestead economy was neglected until late in development (anonymous, unverified).
- Players complained about the Accounting Book being hard to understand (multiple GameFAQs and Steam threads asking how it works) and bugs (the 80% routes).
- Players do not seem to have complained about the system as a grind in the main reviews. The complaints were about the game's pacing and missions instead.
- Importance: effectively optional, but the game's money is thin elsewhere, so most players dipped in.

Black Flag fleet:
- Opinions split. One forum user called it an afterthought (no interactive battles, no ship customisation). Another said most of your cash comes from it. A social post called naval combat and the fleet the favourite part.
- Player aggregators list "non-functional Kenway's Fleet" among complaints, probably about online dependence.
- Game Informer's review wanted more ways to earn upgrade resources.
- Importance: optional, passive money.

Rogue fleet: very little opinion found.

Pattern across all three: the systems are loved by players who want a passive income, ignored by others, and criticised for being shallow (few decisions) rather than hard.

## 5. Comparison with Naval's current design

Naval, per `EconomySpec.md` and `VerticalSlice.md`, is a full trading game, not a side minigame: the whole game is route trading across 12 ports and 14 goods. The AC games had trade as a money layer on top of action games.

| Topic | Assassin's Creed source | Naval now |
|---|---|---|
| Role of trading | Optional income layer | The whole game |
| Goods | Crafted from raw material by artisans | 14 bought and sold goods with port-specific prices |
| Prices | Fixed per merchant, merchant multipliers | Beginner fixed prices with spreads; Veteran reactive prices and stock |
| Spread | Convoys pay a multiple over base price | Buy/sell spread (0.60 to 0.10 by port level) |
| Capacity | Slots (up to 8 wagon, 15 ship) | Hold space with bulky and compact crates |
| Fleet | 2 or 3 convoys; Black Flag up to 15 ships | Unlimited so far (open issue) |
| Travel time | Real time, 12 to 22 minutes (land); hours in Black Flag | In-game, continuous, 1.5 seconds per day |
| Route risk | A percentage per route, lowered by missions | Hazards per route per day, seasons, hull and guns upgrades |
| Route access | Visit shops, craft convoys | Buy routes with gold |
| Growth | More artisans, recipes, slots | Port levels, buildings, ships, upgrades |
| Loss | Convoy lost, replace it | Ship and cargo lost at 0 hull |
| UI | One Accounting Book | Map, port screens |

### Kept in spirit
- Send cargo out and be paid when it arrives.
- Risk against reward on route choice, with a way to improve routes.
- Capacity matters, and growing it is a main upgrade path.
- Long-term growth of a base (Homestead to ports and buildings).
- Money builds your capability to trade in better places.

### What Naval does differently
- The player chooses what to buy and sell. In AC the goods are made, not traded.
- Goods and routes are many, not a few.
- Prices can move with the player's trades (Veteran).
- The player sees ships travel and meets events.
- The fleet is part of the play.

### Ideas to adapt in Naval's own way
1. **Risk as a visible percentage per route.** AC3 shows one readable number. Naval's hazards are per day and hidden in CSVs. Show a route's expected loss chance for a voyage in the route picker, split by storm, pirates and season.
2. **Clearing routes to lower risk.** AC3's three missions, 25 points each, to a floor of 5%, give a clear goal. Naval could add convoy escorts, a patrol ship, or a port "lighthouse" or "harbour guard" building that cuts hazards along connected routes. This fits the existing building slots.
3. **A risk floor.** A 5% minimum keeps risk alive. Naval could keep some residual hazard so insurance or buildings never remove it.
4. **Fixed prices for the early game.** Already in Beginner. AC3's fixed multipliers per merchant are what make its numbers easy to plan around.
5. **Real-time convoy returns for a calm mode.** Use only if Naval wants a passive mode. The in-game clock already serves this role.
6. **Capacity growth as a purchase.** AC3 sells capacity upgrades. Naval's Hold upgrades do this.
7. **Per-merchant multipliers.** AC3 has merchants pay different multiples. Naval's port levels and spread already do this per port.
8. **Reroll and shortlist.** Rogue's re-randomised mission list could inspire a daily "contracts" board.
9. **Cargo gates ship choice.** Black Flag missions need a minimum cargo. Naval could offer contracts that need a given hold space, so ship size matters.
10. **One screen for the whole business.** AC3's ledger is confusing, but the idea of one place for stock, routes and ships is good. Make Naval's version clear.
11. **Mission lists as unlock steps.** Naval's campaign missions could include route-clearing tasks that cut hazards, like privateer contracts.

### Pitfalls to avoid
- **A hidden or confusing UI.** AC3's Accounting Book generated many "how do I use this" threads. Explain risk, capacity and profit on the screen.
- **Bugs that leave risk stuck.** Make sure risk values update visibly after an action.
- **Loss with no player response.** AC3's naval convoy losses had no defence option. Naval's pirate and guns system already gives some agency, so keep it.
- **One dominant route.** AC3's bear-pelt-to-Saint-Augustine trick shows that a single best trade appears. Naval's open issue 4 (Beginner as a one-route game) is the same trap. Fixed prices and no stock make it worse.
- **Money with nothing to buy.** AC3's economy was called pointless because the player quickly had more money than uses. Keep sinks (buildings, upkeep, ships).
- **Waiting as the main cost.** Black Flag's hours-long timers are a real-time gate. If Naval ever adds one, keep it short or optional.
- **Shallow decisions.** The AC fleets were criticised for having few choices. Keep choices frequent in Naval.
- **Unbounded fleet size.** Black Flag capped ships at about 15 with docks, AC3 capped convoys at 2 or 3. Naval's open issue 1 (no fleet limit in Beginner) needs a similar cap, probably as dock or berth slots in port levels or buildings.
- **Trusting fan numbers.** Many AC figures here are single-source. Do not tune Naval to them. Use them for shape only.
- **Borrowed art.** VerticalSlice.md already flags the map art as another game's screenshot. Keep all references to AC for design only.
