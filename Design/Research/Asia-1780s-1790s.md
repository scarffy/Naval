# Asia Chapters Research: Southeast Asia (1780s) and East Asia (1790s)

Purpose: design input for two later "Naval" chapters. The game is inspired by history, not a faithful recreation, so the aim is plausibility and flavour. Money is "gold".

Method note: this file comes from about 24 web searches. Full pages from en.wikipedia.org and samurai-archives.com could not be opened, so everything here rests on search summaries. Several results were weak (hobbyist wikis, machine-generated encyclopaedias). Treat figures as approximate. Anything marked "(unverified)" is from memory or from a single weak source. Gameplay numbers (distances, speeds, prices) are design suggestions, not history.

## 1. Southeast Asia (about 1780s)

### 1.1 Historical frame

- The Dutch VOC was weakening. The Fourth Anglo-Dutch War (1780-84) exposed this, and the VOC was dissolved in 1799. Batavia and Java stayed Dutch. The British took Malacca in 1795 (held to 1818).
- The Bugis of Riau and Selangor challenged the Dutch, mainly over tin. Raja Haji attacked Malacca in 1784 and was killed there. The Dutch then occupied Riau and built a fort at Tanjung Pinang (accounts of the 1784 fighting conflict on dates). The Johor-Riau court moved to Daik-Lingga around 1787-88.
- The British EIC founded Penang in 1786 (Francis Light, from Kedah) as a free port to draw trade away from the Dutch.
- Chinese demand for tea (boosted by the 1784 Commutation Act) pulled Southeast Asian goods toward Canton: tin, pepper, trepang, birds' nests, sharkfin, camphor. This is the engine of the whole region. Sulu's slave raiding grew with it (Warren dates the Iranun phenomenon 1768-1878).

4. **Riau (Tanjung Pinang)** - Bugis-Malay entrepot, the Dutch fight, smuggling.

### 1.6 Seasons and hazards

- **Monsoon timing (approximate; sources differ by a month or two).** Northeast monsoon: about November to March or April, winds from the north-east. This is the fair season at the western end of the Strait. Southwest monsoon: about May to September (some sources say April to November on the Sumatra coast), winds from the south-west. Transitional months are October and April-May. Inside the Strait winds are variable with land and sea breezes. The south-west monsoon "seldom blows far into the strait" (middle Strait often calm), and "Sumatra" squalls from the south-west are more common in the south-west monsoon. Surface current in the Strait runs north-west in both seasons.
- **Java Sea and Makassar:** north-west (wet) monsoon blows roughly December to March toward Australia and Timor. This is how Makassan trepang fleets went to Marege and came back on the dry-season off-shore winds. (Source: reporting on Makassan voyages.)
- **Typhoons:** the South China Sea typhoon season is mainly May to September (July-September storms tend to move north-west then west toward northern Vietnam). They matter for Manila, Jolo-Manila, and Riau-Manila legs, and far more for the East Asia chapter. Typhoons rarely reach the equatorial Malay waters. (Latitude point is general knowledge, unverified here.)
- **Doldrums:** equatorial calms around Singapore Strait and the Strait's middle section, mostly in monsoon changeover. (general knowledge, unverified)
- **Iranun and Balangingi raiders:** based in Mindanao and Sulu. Warren frames the Iranun era as 1768-1878. In the 1780s Iranun lived in organised colonies and raided in the Philippines, Borneo, Java Sea and the Strait. There was no drop in the 1790s, only a change of targets. Balangingi became prominent later (1840s). Raiders used lanong and garay ships (see 1.8). Raiding sought captives for sale in Sulu, a major part of the Sulu economy. Present this factually. Raiders in the game should be a risk to cargo and ship, not a source of "slave goods".
- **Strait pirates:** Malay sea-people (orang laut) and Bugis/Malay chiefs near Riau and the Karimun-Lingga islands had piracy and privateering tied to politics. (general, partly unverified.) Use as a lower-level hazard than Sulu.

### 1.7 Trade rules and mechanics

- **Dutch spice monopoly.** Cloves limited to Ambon and nutmeg to Banda after the 1621 conquest, enforced by hongi patrols (fleets of kora-kora boats) that destroyed trees grown elsewhere. Forced labour and violence underlay this, including the killing or enslavement of the Banda population in 1621. By the late 18th century enforcement weakened as the VOC went bankrupt, smuggling grew, and seedlings left the islands (a Frenchman took nutmeg seedlings to Mauritius from 1769). Game use: Ambon and Banda sell spices cheaply but only to licensed buyers. Smuggling elsewhere is possible but with patrol risk.
- **EIC "country trade".** Private traders licensed by the Company carried Indian goods (cotton, later opium) east and brought back tin, pepper, and Southeast Asian goods to Canton, where the Company took payment in silver or bills. Penang and Riau were prime country-trade stops.
- **Bugis networks.** Bugis traders and sailing nakodas ran Makassar - Riau - Selangor - Borneo - Moluccas routes, bypassing Dutch control and tin contracts. Game use: Bugis shipping gives a "free port" discount at Riau and Makassar.
- **Opium.** Factually: by the 1780s-90s, Bengal opium carried by country traders was reaching Southeast Asia and China, about 4,000 chests a year by about 1789-90 (sources differ), and the Qing banned smoking in 1796 and importing in 1799/1800. **Recommendation: leave opium out as a good.** Keep it as background flavour in port descriptions only, or replace it with "Bengal goods" or "Indian cloth" in the market. This avoids making players run the trade.

### 1.9 Suggested signature mechanic: the Monsoon Wheel

- A visible calendar splits the year into NE monsoon (Nov-Mar), transition (Apr-May, Oct), and SW monsoon (May-Sep).
- Routes have a "wind quality" per direction and month. Penang to Batavia runs fast one way and slow the other. At the monsoon flip ships mid-voyage find the wind turned. The player must plan departure so the flip helps rather than strands them.
- Strait middle calms in the SW monsoon (slow, but raiders see you). Typhoon risk on the Manila legs from May to September.
- Raider pressure rises on routes near Sulu and the Borneo coast in the NE monsoon. (design choice)
- This echoes hurricane season: mid-mission change to plans.

## 2. East Asia (about 1790s)

### 2.1 Historical frame

- **Canton System.** Western trade restricted to Canton, through licensed Cohong merchants (guild formed 1760, membership changed). Foreign ships anchored at Whampoa. Merchants lived in the Thirteen Factories only for the trading season and withdrew to Macau afterward. Silver was the main payment. Tea was the key export. The 1784 Commutation Act cut British tea tax from about 119 percent to 12.5 percent, so demand surged.
- **Japan.** Only Dutch and Chinese ships at Nagasaki. Dutch were confined to Dejima. A 1715 rule set about 30 Chinese and 2 Dutch ships a year. In 1790 the Dutch were cut to 1 ship (some sources say, back to 2 in 1799) with a copper cap (EB1911 says about 350 tons, unverified). Chinese were cut to 10 ships a year in 1790 under Matsudaira Sadanobu. The Dutch Edo visits fell from yearly to once every four years. Copper and dried sea products had replaced silver and gold as Japan's main exports.
- **Ryukyu.** A kingdom paying tribute to China (trade at the Ryukyu House in Fuzhou) while controlled by Satsuma, which used it for covert trade, including supplying kelp and sea products Ryukyu needed for China.
- **Korea.** Tsushima's Sō family traded at the Busan trading post (waegwan): silver out, ginseng and raw silk in. By the late 1700s silver was giving way to copper, Japanese ginseng growing reduced demand, and Tsushima had money troubles.
- **Vietnam.** Hoi An was declining by the 1770s-1780s (Tay Son rebellion from 1771 and silting). Pirates from the South China coast allied with the Tay Son from about 1790 to 1802.
- **Pirates.** The big Chinese pirate confederation grew mainly around 1800-1810 (Zheng Yi, about 600-1,800 ships depending on source, died 1807). Late 1790s is the start. Zheng Qi was recruited by the Tay Son in 1788.

### 2.6 Seasons and hazards

- **Monsoons.** NE monsoon about October to March (cold, dry, from the north-east): favours ships going south and west from China. SW monsoon about April or May to September: favours ships going north from Southeast Asia to China and Japan. Chinese junks from Southeast Asia typically arrived with the SW monsoon and left with the NE. (general knowledge, partly unverified.)
- **Canton season.** Western ships arrived with the SW monsoon (about July-September), traded through the autumn and left with the NE monsoon (about December-February), with merchants retiring to Macau for the off-season. (approximate, from general Canton-trade knowledge and the confirmed season/Macau rule).
- **Typhoons.** May to September, peak July-September. Storms tend to move north-west toward south China and Vietnam. Dangerous for Macau, Canton approaches, Amoy, Manila and Ryukyu legs. Zheng Yi himself may have died in a typhoon in 1807 (accounts differ).
- **Pirates.** Late 1790s: South China Sea pirate fleets allied with the Tay Son (Zheng Qi from 1788, confederation growing to 1805). Peak is after the chapter (1800-1810), so keep the 1790s level "growing".
- **Chinese coastal enforcement.** Qing patrol junks and customs inspections in Canton.

### 2.7 Trade rules and mechanics

- **Canton System.** Western ships may trade only at Canton (anchoring at Whampoa), only through Cohong merchants, only in season, and want silver. Players must pay port fees (a "measurement fee" and "gifts" (unverified)), cannot bring foreign women into the factories (general), and winter at Macau.
- **Japan's restrictions.** Only Dutch (Dejima) and Chinese (Tojin yashiki) at Nagasaki, with annual ship limits and, for Dutch, a copper cap. 1790: Dutch cut to 1 ship, Chinese to 10 (single-source). Use "tally" permits: the player needs a licence to enter Nagasaki and the number of permits per year is small. Fits a "limited slots" mechanic.
- **Ryukyu and Korea.** Naha works through China tribute (Fuzhou); Busan through Tsushima only. Treat both as "closed" ports reachable only through a quest or a licence.
- **Country trade into Canton.** British country ships bring Indian cotton and other goods to Canton and sell for silver or bills. Silver outflow is the reason Britain sought other goods. Leave opium out (see below).
- **Opium.** Factually: the Qing banned smoking in 1796 and importing in 1799/1800, yet smuggling continued; Americans joined in the 1780s; Portuguese Macau ships carried opium by the 1770s. Opium storage moved from Macau to Whampoa then Lintin (Lintin about 1820). **Recommendation: no opium good.** Replace with "Bengal cotton" or "Indian goods" as the country-trade import, and mention the real trade only in codex text.

### 2.9 Suggested signature mechanic: the Canton Season (with a typhoon overlay)

- The chapter's clock revolves around the trading season. Western-flagged ships can only sell at Canton during open months and must hold a Cohong "security merchant" (a licence slot). After the season closes, the player must withdraw to Macau.
- Nagasaki permits work the same way, with a very small yearly quota.
- Typhoon season (May-Sept) overlays: a ship caught at sea in the wrong month risks damage or delay. This gives a mid-mission change like hurricane season.
- Alternative simpler pick: "Licence slots" alone as the signature mechanic, with typhoons as a weather hazard.

## 3. How the two chapters connect

- **Malacca Strait.** The gate between Indian Ocean and China. Penang and Riau are the natural last ports of chapter SEA before sailing east.
- **South China Sea and the junk trade.** The route Riau/Batavia - (Pontianak) - Manila or Macau/Canton is the main spine. Chinese junks came to Riau, Batavia, Pontianak and Jolo yearly and returned with tin, pepper, camphor, trepang, birds' nests and sharkfin. This explains why the SEA chapter's trepang, birds' nests and tin are wanted at Amoy and Canton.
- **Country trade and silver.** Southeast Asian goods sold at Canton paid for tea. Silver flowed from Manila and Mexico into China.
- **Manila** is the shared port: Spanish, galleon link, and the junk trade with Amoy.
- **Suggested World mode link.**
  1. Open a "Strait to China" route from Riau (or Penang) to Macau/Canton, long, monsoon-timed (leave Riau in the SW monsoon, return in the NE).
  2. Chapter 1's Atlantic spice and tea demand can drive cargo: tea, porcelain and silk flow west, silver and spices flow east. A Cape route from Europe to Batavia or Canton is a possible later link (long, not covered here).
  3. Monsoon Wheel and Canton Season share one calendar: Strait monsoon flip meets the Canton open window, so a combined voyage Penang to Canton has to be timed.
  4. Keep typhoon and raider hazards regional so the combined map reads clearly.
- **Ship carry-over.** Junks and country ships appear in both chapters. Padewakang is SEA only. Fujian junk is shared.
